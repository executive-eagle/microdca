What is bitcoin?

Digital money that no government or bank controls, with a fixed supply of 21 million coins.

You don't have to buy a whole one. Bitcoin splits into 100,000,000 tiny pieces called "sats", so $10 buys a small slice.

Its price swings a lot. It has fallen more than 50% several times, and it has also grown many times over in the long run. That's why people often buy a little at a time instead of all at once.

Try buying a little every week

Buying a little at a time

Invest the same dollar amount on a schedule, no matter what the price is.

This is called dollar cost averaging (DCA). Say you buy $25 of bitcoin every week. When the price is low, your $25 buys more. When it's high, it buys less. Over time you end up paying an average price, and you never have to guess the perfect moment to buy.

It won't stop you from losing money if prices fall and stay down. And if prices only go up, putting everything in on day one would have done better. Its real strength is that it's easy to stick with.

Dividends and payouts

Cash some investments pay you just for owning them.

Payouts are paid per share. If a share pays 10% a year on $100, you get $10 a year for each share, split into monthly, quarterly or even daily payments.

The yearly payout divided by the price you pay is called the yield. "Yield on cost" is your yearly income divided by the money you put in. Payouts aren't promised: companies can lower them, skip them or stop them.

Preferred stock (STRC, SATA, STRF, STRK, STRD)

A share that pays a set dividend and gets paid before regular stock, but after lenders.

Strategy (the company formerly called MicroStrategy) and Strive sell preferred stock and use the money to buy bitcoin. Each share has a $100 "stated amount" the dividend is based on, but its market price can be above or below $100.

Cumulative means a skipped payment is still owed later (STRF, STRK). Non-cumulative means a skipped payment is gone for good (STRD). These shares are not backed by the company's bitcoin. If the company runs into trouble, payouts can stop and prices can fall.

Strategy's shareholders vote on Oct 28, 2026 on paying dividends every day instead of monthly or quarterly. MicroDCA's "Daily (proposed)" setting shows what that would look like.

Covered-call ETFs (BTCI)

Funds that give up some future growth in exchange for cash today.

An ETF is a basket of investments you can buy like one stock. A covered-call ETF sells other investors the right to buy its holdings at a set price, collects a fee for that, and pays the fee out to you every month. That's how BTCI pays such a high rate.

The catch: when bitcoin jumps, these funds don't keep all of the gain, and over time their price has tended to drift down. A big payout rate isn't the same as making money. Always look at what the whole investment is worth, not just the income.

Compounding

Reinvesting your payouts so your payouts start earning payouts.

Put $1,000 into something paying 10% a year. Take the payouts as cash and after 10 years you've collected $1,000 on top of your $1,000, so $2,000 total. Reinvest every payout and you'd have about $2,594, because each year's payout is a little bigger than the last.

The catch: compounding only helps while the investment keeps paying and its price holds up.

Margin (borrowing to invest)

Borrowing money from your broker, using your investments as the guarantee.

Margin makes gains bigger and losses bigger, and you pay interest the whole time. If prices drop too far, the broker can sell your investments for you at the worst moment (a "margin call").

Paying down a margin loan is like earning a guaranteed return equal to the interest rate. MicroDCA's payout splitter (Advanced mode) lets you send part of your income to paying it off.

Questions

Is this real money?
No. Everything on MicroDCA is a simulation. Nothing is bought or sold, and you can't lose or make money here.
Where do the numbers come from?
Real daily closing prices and real past dividend payments from Yahoo Finance. Prices refresh every 15 seconds while the stock market is open, and bitcoin's price comes live from Coinbase.
Is this financial advice?
No. It's a learning tool. Past results don't predict the future. Talk to a licensed professional before investing.
Can I invest through MicroDCA?
No. To actually buy any of these, you'd use a brokerage account. Some brokers let you buy fractions of a share or schedule recurring buys; others don't, especially for preferred stock.
What about taxes?
MicroDCA ignores taxes and fees. Strategy has said its recent dividends were treated as "return of capital", which is taxed differently than normal income. A tax professional can explain what that means for you.
What's the difference between Simple and Advanced?
Simple shows the essentials in plain English. Advanced shows every setting: exact dates, custom splits, adding your own tickers, sending payouts to margin or bills, and more detailed stats.
